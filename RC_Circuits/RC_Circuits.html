RC Circuits

1 Notation and Equations

  • We use the the letter “C” as a label for a capacitor and the units of charge. When used as a label, C is usually written in italics. Thus

    “QC​=10 C” means “the capacitor labeled C has a charge of 10 Coulombs.”

  • Lower case letters are used for electrical quantities that vary in time. In circuits constant current and voltages, we used I and V. In the circuits considered in this activity, the currents and voltages vary in time, so we use i(t), q(t), and v(t).

  • In this activity, we use the the relationship i(t)=dq(t)/dt between the current i(t) in the wires connected to a capacitor and the charge q(t) on the capacitor.

2 Discharging Capacitor

If one capacitor with capacitance C is in a circuit with other resistors (and no emfs), the charge on the capacitor changes with time according to

q(t)=Qo​e−t/τ,

where τ≡RC, Qo​ is the charge on the capacitor at t=0, and R is the equivalent resistance. The quantity τ is called the “RC time constant.” The equation for q(t) is derived by solving the differential equation that follows from using Kirchhoff’s voltage law.

Question: According to the rate at which q(t) decays is lower when the resistance is larger. Justify this physically.

For infinite resistance, there will be no current and so the charge on the capacitor will not decay.

3 Charging a Capacitor

If a single uncharged capacitor is in series with other resistors and a DC voltage source that is turned on at t=0, the charge of the capacitor varies according to

q(t)=Qf​(1−e−t/τ)

where τ≡RC, R is the equivalent resistance, and Qf​ is the final charge on the capacitor, that is, the charge on the capacitor as t→∞ (it is technically more accurate to say t/τ→∞ instead of t→∞; why?).

Answer: t is a number with a unit such as seconds. Infinity is a number without a unit. Also, when we say “large time”, we should always qualify what large is relative to. For example, “one day” is long to an insect because its lifespan is about a day; to a dog, “one day” is not long relative to its lifespan.

To find Qf​, replace the capacitor with an open circuit and use Kirchhoff’s voltage law to find the voltage across the capacitor, Vf​, and then use the relationship Qf​=CVf​.

3.1 Example

Find Qf​ and then q(t) for the following circuit, assuming the switch is closed at t=0 and the capacitor is initially uncharged.

Answer: When the switch is closed, charge builds up on the capacitor. This build–up continues until the charge on the capacitor is such that no current flows in the circuit. If no current flows through the capacitor, the circuit is equivalent to one in which the capacitor is replaced with an open circuit, as shown below.

Kirchhoff’s voltage Law gives E−i(t)R−vC​(t)=0, where vC​(t) is the voltage across the capacitor. For large t, i(t)=0, so vC​(t)=E. This voltage is for large t, so we relabel it as Vf​. Using Qf​=CVf​ gives Qf​=CE, so

q(t)=CE(1−e−t/RC)

Note that the general formula q(t)=Qf​(1−e−t/τ) that applies for all t is derived by solving the differential equation E−i(t)R−vC​(t)=0 using i(t)=dq(t)/dt and vC​(t)=q(t)/C.

4 Charge/Discharge Problem

4.1 Part I

The switch in the above circuit has been in position a for a long time. At t=0, the switch is moved instantaneously to position b. The values of the circuit elements are E=12 V, C=10 mF, and R=20 Ω.

Let t=0− correspond to the time just before the switch is moved from a to b. Let t=0+ correspond to the time just after the switch is moved from a to b.

  1. What is the current through the resistor at t=0−?

    Answer: Zero. The battery charges the capacitor until the charge on the capacitor is such that it is equivalent to a battery with an emf of E oriented such that no current flows through it.

  2. What is the charge on the capacitor at t=0−?

    Answer: After a long time in position a, the current is zero. Replacing the capacitor with an open circuit and using KVL gives vC​=E. So q(0−)=CvC​(0−)=(10 mF)(12 V)=120 mC.

  3. What is the charge on the capacitor at t=0+?

    Answer: 120 mC

  4. What is the voltage across the capacitor at t=0+?

    Answer: Using V=Q/C gives vC​(0+)=120 mC/(10 mF)=12 V=E

  5. What is the current through the resistor at t=0+?

    Answer: Immediately after the switch is moved to b, the capacitor starts to discharge though the resistor. Kirchhoff’s Voltage law for the new closed loop states that the voltage across the resistor equals the voltage across the capacitor, vC​. By Ohm’s law current through the resistor is i(0+)=vC​(0+)/R=12 V/20 Ω=0.6 A.

  6. What is the charge on the capacitor at t=200 ms?

    Answer:  The time constant is RC=(10 mF)(20 Ω)=200 ms. At t=200 ms, the capacitor is in the process of discharging. The charge on the capacitor varies according to q(t)=Qo​e−t/RC, so

    q(200 ms)=Q0​e−200 ms/RC=120 mC⋅e−200 ms/200 ms≃120 mC⋅0.37≃44 mC.

 

4.2 Part II

The switch in the above circuit has been in position b for a long time. At t=0, the switch is moved instantaneously to position a. The values of the circuit elements are E=12 V, C=10 mF, and R=20 Ω.

Let t=0− correspond to the time just before the switch is moved from b to a. Let t=0+ correspond to the time just after the switch is moved from b to a.

  1. What is the current through the resistor at t=0−?

    Answer:  Since the switch is on position b for a long time, the charge on and voltage across the capacitor is zero. If the voltage across the capacitor is zero, then by Kirchhoff’s voltage law, the voltage across the resistor must be zero. So the current through the resistor is zero; iC​(0−)=0.

  2. What is the voltage across the capacitor at t=0−?

    Answer:  vC​(0−)=0. This follows from KVL, i(0−)R−vC​(0−)=0, and the fact that i(0−)=0 because the circuit had the switch at b for a long time so that the capacitor was fully discharged.

  3. What is the charge on the capacitor at (a) t=0− and (b) t=0+?

    Answer:  (a) Zero; (b) Zero

  4. What is the voltage across the capacitor at t=0+?

    Answer:  vC​(0+)=0.

  5. What is the current through the resistor at t=0+?

    Answer: Kirchhoff’s voltage law at this time is

    E−i(0+)R−vC​(0+)=0

    At t=0+ the voltage across the capacitor is zero because it is uncharged, so i(0+)R=E. Thus, i(0+)=E/R=12 V/20 Ω=0.6 A.

  6. What is the charge on the capacitor at time t=200 ms?

    Answer:  The time constant is RC=200 ms. At t=200 ms, the charge accumulated on the capacitor is:

    q(200 s)=CE(1−e−(200 s)/RC)=(10 mF⋅12 V)(1−e−200/200)≃(120 mV)(0.63)≃75.9 mV
